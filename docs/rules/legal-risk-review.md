## Summary

**Risk register:** 42 items. 10 High, 27 Medium, 5 Low.

**Most important findings, ranked:**

1. **"Jurisdiction" is undefined, and it drives everything (R1, R37–R39).**
   - The facts carry one `jurisdiction` plus `decedent_resident_of_jurisdiction`.
   - The law turns on several different connecting factors:
     - **Domicile.** CA Civ. Code § 946; CA quasi-community property under Prob. Code § 101 "domiciled in this state"; WA residency for RCW 11.62.010(2)(b).
     - **Where the property is.** CA § 13100 "property in this state".
     - **Where the institution does business.** RCW 30A.22.040(8); *Anderson Nat'l Bank v. Luckett*, "according to the law of the state where it does business".
   - The engine must record these separately. If they point to different states, it must return `not_determinable`, never a guess.
2. **Federal benefits credited after death can make the institution liable for the whole balance it paid out (R2).**
   - 31 CFR § 210.10(a) makes the receiving bank (RDFI) liable for "all benefit payments received after the death" unless it limits liability under § 210.11. Limited liability depends on the bank having no actual or constructive knowledge of the death.
   - On Path A the engine releases the full balance "on request" once proof of death is in. By that point the institution knows of the death.
   - Post-death federal credits need a fact, plus a hold or return step, before any payout.
3. **"Stricter-only" policy is not legally risk-free (R3).**
   - `MAX_ADDITIONAL_WAITING_DAYS = 3650` lets an institution add up to ten years. CA Prob. Code § 13105(b) awards the claimant attorney's fees when a holder "acted unreasonably in refusing to pay … within a reasonable time".
   - RCW 11.62.010(1) says the holder "shall pay". UDAAP's unfairness test (12 U.S.C. § 5531(c)) is also relevant.
   - "Stricter" therefore means more exposure to the claimant, not less. Policy limits need attorney review.
4. **Survival rules differ by state, and the schema can't express them (R4).**
   - WA requires survival by 120 hours for POD designations and co-owners (RCW 11.05A.030, .040).
   - CA's 120-hour rule (§ 6403) applies to intestacy. For accounts, CA uses "clear and convincing evidence" of survival with no fixed period (§§ 220–223; § 21109 for instruments).
   - A single `survived_decedent: bool` cannot encode either rule.
5. **The statutory discharges protect payments that comply with the statute, not reliance on software (R23).**
   - CA § 13106(a) applies only "if the requirements of Section 13100 to 13104 … are satisfied". § 5405(a) covers payments "made pursuant to" §§ 5401–5404.
   - If the engine is wrong, or the facts entered don't match the documents, the institution loses the discharge. Vendor contract terms cannot restore it.
6. **Blocking events missing from the schema (R5–R8, R11–R14, R33).**
   - The current fact schema has no way to record:
     - a slayer or abuser notice;
     - an elder-exploitation hold;
     - an IRS levy;
     - a garnishment;
     - a bankruptcy;
     - an OFAC match;
     - a lien, pledge, or setoff on the account;
     - knowledge that an affidavit is false.
   - Each can block or change payment under the authority cited below.
   - The engine's "determined" output should either state these as preconditions it did not check, or collect facts for them.
7. **Regulatory status of the product (R25–R27).**
   - Third-party risk management (TPRM) guidance applies. The 2023 interagency guidance is in force, and a 2026 proposal to replace it has comments due 2026-11-16.
   - Model risk management guidance probably does **not** apply. SR 26-2 (April 17, 2026) superseded SR 11-7, and its definition of "model" excludes "deterministic rule-based processes and software".
   - Once hosted, the vendor may be a CFPB "service provider" directly subject to the UDAAP prohibition (12 U.S.C. §§ 5481(26), 5536(a)(1)(B)).
8. **UPL risk is moderate for a business-to-business tool and higher if anything faces consumers (R22).**
   - In *In re Reynoso*, 477 F.3d 1117 (9th Cir. 2007), the court found UPL under California law where an "expert system" offered "personalized — albeit automated — counsel".
   - The court expressly reserved whether "software alone" is the practice of law.

## Gaps and omissions (cross-cutting)

1. **No federal-overlay preconditions on decisions.** A `determined` decision implies a payout can proceed. In fact several federal steps come first:
   - reclamation review (R2);
   - OFAC screening (R11);
   - levy check (R8);
   - a transfer certificate where needed (R10);
   - BSA identity verification (R19).

   Add a fixed "institution pre-payment checks" list to every determined decision.
2. **Payment-blocking facts are incomplete.** Schema v4 has a restraining order, a withdrawal notice, a dispute notice, and a testamentary-disposition notice. It is missing:
   - slayer or abuser notice;
   - exploitation hold;
   - tax levy or garnishment;
   - bankruptcy;
   - OFAC hit;
   - knowledge of a false affidavit;
   - competing affidavits;
   - encumbrance or setoff;
   - post-death federal credits;
   - reclamation notice;
   - a 120-hour survival measure;
   - payee alive at payment;
   - payee is a minor or lacks capacity;
   - the decedent's estate-tax residency;
   - an aggregate balance;
   - separate domicile, account-office, and governing-law states.
3. **The policy model has no upper bound tied to law** (R3).
4. **No statement of what the engine does not check.** Facts are taken as given, and the statutory discharges depend on the documents. Users need that explained.
